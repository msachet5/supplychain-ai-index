    How this index is built

    What it measures

    Each box is one of 94 supply chain processes, grouped into 13 groups modelled on SAP modules. Each process gets two scores from 0 to 10. AI capability is the share of the work that current AI could do at acceptable quality with typical data. Adoption is how widely AI is actually in production for it across North American companies. The exposure index (0–100) is capability × 10, weighted by wages. The adoption index is the same for adoption.

    Who does the work, and what it costs

    Worker counts and mean wages come from official statistics: for the US, and for Canada (Canadian dollars converted at the ). Each occupation's time is split across processes; for example, logisticians spend about 12% of their time on supply planning. Jobs that are only partly supply chain count only that share (for example, about 15% of customer service reps handle orders). Box area is the total wages for that slice of work.

    Scope

    From buying materials through making, storing, moving and distributing to the customer's door, plus returns. Line workers and distributors are in. Farming, mining, the retail selling floor, R&D and utilities are out.

    Scores and evidence

    Every score has a one-line reason. Scores marked AI first pass were drafted by an AI model and are pending review; hand-scored processes were reviewed by a supply chain practitioner. AI products are examples, not endorsements; their capabilities are as the vendors describe them. Evidence entries are labelled as independent studies, news reports or company-reported figures.

    Limits

    Job codes do not match how people describe their jobs: demand planners have no code of their own and are counted as logisticians. Time splits and partial-job shares are estimates. US employment is for 2025 and Canadian employment for 2023. Mexico is not yet included.
